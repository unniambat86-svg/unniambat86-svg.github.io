About

I spent the first decade of my career deep in engineering — building solutions at Samsung and Sony, then doing my graduate degree in Computer Science at the University of Utah. I understood how things were built, and I understood them well.

But somewhere along the way I realised I was more frustrated by the product decisions being made around me than by any technical problem I was solving. I kept seeing things shipped that shouldn't have been, and gaps nobody was filling. I was tired of watching from the engineering seat — I wanted to own the whole thing. So I moved into product.

When I joined Cisco Meraki, I had two weeks of onboarding and was thrown straight into the deep end — no hand-holding, just fires to fight and a platform to learn at the same time. The first six months were brutal. But instead of rushing to ship features, I focused on understanding what was actually broken first. Quality before novelty. Get the fundamentals right, then introduce things nobody else had. That discipline — and something Dan Miller wrote that stuck with me, that customers don't know what they want until you give it to them — is what turned a product line with zero revenue into a $200M+ business.

I grew up in India, but living in the US changed how I think. That's where I understood what it really means to think without a ceiling, back yourself, and respect people for what they do rather than where they're from. I wouldn't be building what I'm building now without that.

I've since lived in Canada and Germany, visited 30+ countries, and pick up languages compulsively — seven so far. The bigger obsession is macroeconomics and geopolitics. Not as a pastime — I study how power works because I want to eventually be in a position to use it well, and you can't shape what you don't understand. It also helps with the stock market.
